Observation 1 Design systems · strategy FYERS · web + mobile · 8-person team · 2024 — ongoing

FDSG — one design language across a fintech platform

Role: Design Review Board member → owner of v2.0 and v2.1
Setting

Design didn't have one language — it had several dialects. Style guides were scattered, half-finished, or duplicated, so the same element existed in different ways across web, iOS, and Android. That cost the business twice: designers rebuilt instead of designed, and users met a product that felt subtly inconsistent — costly in a product built on trust.

Intervention

I argued for a single, versioned source of truth rather than another guide. After v1.0 I took over and drove FDSG 2.0 and 2.1 across web and mobile — a visual reset from gradient-heavy to clean and minimal, then a pass that tightened spacing and density.

The deeper intervention was organizational: I helped move FYERS from a split UX/UI relay to end-to-end Product Designers, so the people using the system understood it top to bottom.

Note to self Adoption moved with the org change, not the component count. The system was the artefact; the ownership model was the cause.
Contradiction Design-to-code was never a 100% match. Naming that out loud is what kept the system improving instead of quietly rotting.
Next time Capture a baseline before shipping v2.0. The effort saving is real but was measured after the fact.

Observation 2 Real-time trading · execution FYERS · options vertical · Q2 2024 · ~3 months

Options Scalper Terminal — designing for the hardest user in the building

Role: Design lead and oversight
Setting

Most fintech design makes the complex feel simple. This was the opposite: the user wants complexity, at speed, with zero margin for error. High-frequency options traders live in seconds — a slow interaction or an ambiguous state isn't an annoyance, it's money.

Intervention

A fixed layout that becomes muscle memory. Deeper analytics behind progressive disclosure so the default view stays scannable. Live state read through colour and motion rather than full redraws. And error-prevention at speed — a deliberate confirm step and guardrails against fat-finger mistakes.

I set the direction and defended the standard against pressure to surface everything on the default screen.

Principle held The terminal should disappear into muscle memory. Arguable in a stakeholder room because it's a claim about the trader's hands, not my taste.
Sampling bias We validated with the traders we could reach fastest — already the most expert. Muscle memory is a promise to the one who hasn't built it yet.
Not measured Perceived latency. For this user it is the design, and we captured the behaviour it produced rather than the thing itself.

Findings

What held across every site

Enumerated because they're a findings list, not a ranking — each one earned across more than one setting.

F1 Handoffs are where intent dies. One designer should own a problem end to end, from user need to shipped screen. That conviction retired the UX/UI relay at FYERS, and it's how I'd structure any team I lead.
F2 A system is an organizational agreement, not a folder of components. Adoption tracked the ownership model far more closely than it tracked the library.
F3 Name the gap or it rots. Design-to-code was never a 100% match. Saying so kept the system honest; pretending otherwise is how systems quietly decay.
F4 A defensible standard is a claim about the user, not about taste. "The terminal should disappear into muscle memory" survived the stakeholder room precisely because it was about the trader's hands.
F5 Credibility comes from still building. I came in from an engineering foundation — a master's in Advanced Computer Science before one in HCI — and I still ship front-end with AI. It's why the direction I set is arguable in engineering terms too.
F6 Lead the way you wish you'd been led. A clear direction, room to do the best work of your career, and systems that make the next project easier. I care more about the team getting sharper than about being the smartest person in the room.
