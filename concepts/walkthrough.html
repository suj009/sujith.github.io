02Project one · strategy & scale

Six dialects, one language

FDSG — the FYERS design system, v2.0 and v2.1
Company
FYERS · retail broking
Team
8 designers, web + mobile
Timeframe
2024 — ongoing
My role
Review Board → owner, v2.0 & v2.1
Problem
What was actually broken

Design didn't have one language — it had several. Style guides were scattered, half-finished, or duplicated, so the same element existed in different ways across web, iOS, and Android.

That cost the business twice: designers rebuilt instead of designed, and users met a product that felt subtly inconsistent — costly in a product built on trust.

Process
How I framed it and moved it

I argued for a single, versioned source of truth rather than another guide. After v1.0 I took over and drove 2.0 and 2.1 — a visual reset from gradient-heavy to clean and minimal, then a pass that tightened spacing and density.

The harder half was organizational. I helped move FYERS from a split UX/UI relay to end-to-end Product Designers, so the people using the system understood it top to bottom. And when engineering built it into code, we said out loud that design-to-code was never a 100% match — naming the gap is what kept the system improving instead of quietly rotting.

Outcome
What changed
~30%
Less design effort, measured on live projects with vs. without the system
1

UX/UI → PD

If I ran it again
The honest part

I'd instrument adoption from day one instead of arguing it later. The ~30% is real, but I measured it after the fact on live projects — a baseline captured before v2.0 shipped would have made the case in a single slide rather than a conversation. I'd also version the code side in lockstep from the start; letting the design and engineering versions drift is how the design-to-code gap became a thing we had to name at all.

03Project two · execution under pressure

The terminal should disappear

Options Scalper Terminal — designing for the hardest user in the building
Company
FYERS · options vertical
Team
Cross-functional, design + eng
Timeframe
Q2 2024 · ~3 months
My role
Design lead & oversight
Problem
Why this one was different

Most fintech design makes the complex feel simple. This was the opposite: the user wants complexity, at speed, with zero margin for error.

High-frequency options traders live in seconds. A slow interaction or an ambiguous state isn't an annoyance — it's money.

Process
The calls I made and defended

A fixed layout that becomes muscle memory. Deeper analytics behind progressive disclosure so the default view stays scannable. Live state read through colour and motion rather than full redraws. And error-prevention at speed — a deliberate confirm step and guardrails against fat-finger mistakes.

There was real pressure to surface more on the default screen. The principle I held: the terminal should disappear into muscle memory. That's arguable in a stakeholder room because it's a claim about the trader's hands, not about my taste.

Outcome
How the users answered
↑ engagement
Session duration up; MAU grew steadily among active traders
high
Daily order volume per user — a tool people live in
↑ orders
Order volume through the terminal grew at scale

If I ran it again
The honest part

Three months meant we validated the layout with the traders we could reach fastest — who were already the most expert. I'd widen that group earlier, because "muscle memory" is a promise to the trader who hasn't built it yet, and we optimised hardest for the ones who had. I'd also have pushed for latency instrumentation alongside the usage metrics: for this user, perceived speed is the design, and we measured the behaviour it produced rather than the thing itself.

06Closing

What I'd need from you

A seat where design decisions are made rather than reviewed. A mandate that covers the system as well as the screens. And a business honest about what it's actually optimising for.

Give me those and I'll take responsibility for the number, not just the interface.

I optimize for clarity and ownership — I'd rather a designer own a problem end to end than pass it down a relay, because handoffs are where intent dies. I try to lead the way I wish I'd been led: a clear direction, room to do the best work of your career, and systems that make the next project easier.
