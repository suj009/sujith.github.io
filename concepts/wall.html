One language, not six dialects

When I joined FYERS, design didn't have one language — it had several. Style guides were scattered, half-finished, or duplicated, so the same element existed in different ways across web, iOS, and Android. That cost the business twice: designers rebuilt instead of designed, and users met a product that felt subtly inconsistent — costly in a product built on trust.

I argued for a single, versioned source of truth. After v1.0 I took over and drove FDSG 2.0 and 2.1 — a visual reset from gradient-heavy to clean and minimal, then a pass that tightened spacing and density.

The system wasn't the real change. The org was. I helped move FYERS from a split UX/UI relay to end-to-end Product Designers, which made adoption fast because the people using the system understood it top to bottom.

The terminal should disappear

Most fintech design makes the complex feel simple. This was the opposite: the user wants complexity, at speed, with zero margin for error. High-frequency options traders live in seconds — a slow interaction or an ambiguous state isn't an annoyance, it's money.

A fixed layout that becomes muscle memory. Deeper analytics behind progressive disclosure so the default view stays scannable. Live state read through colour and motion rather than full redraws. Error-prevention at speed — a deliberate confirm step and guardrails against fat-finger mistakes.

That was the standard I held against pressure to show everything. It showed up in the behaviour of the most demanding traders on the platform: they leaned in.
