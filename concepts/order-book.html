FDSG — one design language across a fintech platform

Design Review Board member → owner of v2.0 & v2.1

When I joined FYERS, design didn't have one language — it had several dialects. Style guides were scattered, half-finished, or duplicated, so the same element existed in different ways across web, iOS, and Android. That cost the business twice: designers rebuilt instead of designed, and users met a product that felt subtly inconsistent — costly in a product built on trust.

I became one of the louder voices for a single, versioned source of truth rather than more scattered guides. After v1.0, I took over and drove FDSG 2.0 and 2.1 across web and mobile — a visual reset from gradient-heavy to clean and minimal, then a pass that tightened spacing and density. The deeper change was organizational: I helped move FYERS from a split UX/UI relay to end-to-end Product Designers, which made adoption fast because the people using the system understood it top to bottom.

When engineering built it into code, we were honest that design-to-code was never a 100% match — naming the gap is what kept the system improving instead of quietly rotting.

Options Scalper Terminal — designing for the hardest user in the building

Design lead & oversight

Most fintech design makes the complex feel simple. This was the opposite: the user wants complexity, at speed, with zero margin for error. High-frequency options traders live in seconds — a slow interaction or an ambiguous state isn't an annoyance, it's money.

I set the direction and defended the standard: a fixed layout that becomes muscle memory, deeper analytics behind progressive disclosure so the default view stays scannable, live state read through colour and motion rather than full redraws, and error-prevention at speed — a deliberate confirm step and guardrails against fat-finger mistakes.

The principle I held against pressure to show everything: the terminal should disappear into muscle memory. The result showed up in the behaviour of the most demanding traders on the platform — they leaned in, spending more time in the tool and placing more through it.

Career

Ten years, chronological

I'm a product design leader with over ten years turning complex problems into products people enjoy — most of it in fintech, where the stakes and the trust are both high. I came into design from an engineering foundation — a master's in Advanced Computer Science before one in Human-Computer Interaction — which is exactly why "a design leader who ships front-end" isn't a stretch for me. It's where I started.

I optimize for clarity and ownership. I'd rather my team own a problem end to end than pass it down a relay, and I try to lead the way I wish I'd been led: a clear direction, room to do the best work of your career, and systems that make the next project easier.
