Principles

The tokens everything else derives from

A system isn't a folder of components — it's an organizational agreement. Same is true of a design leader. These are the values every decision below resolves to.

ownership.scope One designer, whole problem. From user need to shipped screen. Handoffs are where intent dies, so I killed the UX/UI relay and moved the org to end-to-end product designers.
leadership.mode Player-coach, not spectator. I set direction and defend the standard — and I'll get into the code when it helps. Being hands-on is what makes the direction credible.
truth.sources One, versioned. Not several dialects. Scattered guides cost twice: designers rebuild instead of design, and users meet a product that feels subtly inconsistent.
gap.designToCode Name it, don't hide it. Design-to-code was never a 100% match. Saying so out loud is what kept the system improving instead of quietly rotting.
team.growth Sharper beats smartest. Clear direction, room to do the best work of your career, and systems that make the next project easier. I care more about the team getting sharper than about being the smartest person in the room.

Strategy & leadership Design systems web + mobile · 8-person team

FDSG — one design language across a fintech platform

Design Review Board member → owner of v2.0 & v2.1
Problem

When I joined FYERS, design didn't have one language — it had several dialects. Style guides were scattered, half-finished, or duplicated, so the same element existed in different ways across web, iOS, and Android. That cost the business twice: designers rebuilt instead of designed, and users met a product that felt subtly inconsistent — costly in a product built on trust.

Decision

I became one of the louder voices for a single, versioned source of truth rather than more scattered guides. After v1.0, I took over and drove FDSG 2.0 and 2.1 across web and mobile — a visual reset from gradient-heavy to clean and minimal, then a pass that tightened spacing and density.

The deeper change was organizational: I helped move FYERS from a split UX/UI relay to end-to-end Product Designers. Adoption was fast because the people using the system understood it top to bottom.

Execution & impact Real-time trading Options vertical · Q2 2024 · ~3 months

Options Scalper Terminal — designing for the hardest user in the building

Design lead & oversight
Problem

Most fintech design makes the complex feel simple. This was the opposite: the user wants complexity, at speed, with zero margin for error. High-frequency options traders live in seconds — a slow interaction or an ambiguous state isn't an annoyance, it's money.

Decision

A fixed layout that becomes muscle memory. Deeper analytics behind progressive disclosure so the default view stays scannable. Live state read through colour and motion rather than full redraws. And error-prevention at speed — a deliberate confirm step and guardrails against fat-finger mistakes.

The principle I held against pressure to show everything: the terminal should disappear into muscle memory.

Notes on the author

What it's like to work with me

I came into design from an engineering foundation — a master's in Advanced Computer Science before one in Human-Computer Interaction — which is exactly why "a design leader who ships front-end" isn't a stretch for me. It's where I started.

Today I lead product design at FYERS across six verticals. Before that, DeFi and fintech at Qplum, Datami, Nexter, and Oddz; and before design was my job, I was the person running the interviews and diary studies as an HCI researcher at Microsoft and IBM.

I try to lead the way I wish I'd been led: a clear direction, room to do the best work of your career, and systems that make the next project easier.

I push for craft, I'll get into the code when it helps, and I'd rather my team own a problem end to end than pass it down a relay.
