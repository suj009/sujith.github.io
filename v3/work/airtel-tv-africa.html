Airtel TV Africa: launching a streaming app across a continent

Airtel wanted its video streaming app in 14 African countries. As the only designer on a team of more than 30 engineers, I led the app experience on Android and iOS, and designed the analytics portal Airtel’s own teams ran on.

  • RoleDesign Lead, the only designer
  • Team30+ engineers, a product manager and country leads
  • MarketsAirtel subscribers in 14 African countries
  • Registered users (2020)450,000+ in Nigeria, Zambia and Uganda
01

The brief

Airtel TV gives Airtel’s prepaid and postpaid subscribers unlimited streaming of movies, shows and live TV. Airtel wanted it in 14 African countries, for people aged roughly 16 to 45.

The starting point was the Airtel TV app that had run in India for several years. So the job was not to start from a blank page, but to turn an Indian product into an African one: the features each market asked for, built on an app the engineers already knew, and launched country by country.

02

My role

I was the only designer, leading the experience for the Android and iOS apps alongside more than 30 engineers, an engineering manager, a product manager and the country operating leads.

I took requirements from the product manager, who gathered them from Airtel’s African stakeholders, and walked engineering through every new feature. Twice a week I reviewed progress and delays with the development lead, the product manager, the CTO and the COO.

03

Built for long viewing

Once the first release was safely in the Play Store and App Store, I recommended moving the whole app from a light theme to a dark one. People watch for hours, often in low light: a dark theme reduces eye strain and glare, and saves battery on the OLED screens many of them use. Stakeholders agreed.

I turned the existing screens into a style guide and a reusable component set first, so the switch and every feature after it could be assembled from the same parts instead of redrawn screen by screen.

On top of that I designed and shipped the features the African markets asked for:

  • Choosing the app’s language
  • Content ratings
  • Buying a data bundle from inside the app
  • Renting individual titles (transactional video on demand)
04

Fewer emails, faster partners

Two problems sat outside the app. Content partners sent logos and artwork in whatever size they had, and much of it was unusable, so every new title meant another round of emails. I wrote one set of guidelines for logos and artwork, shared with existing and new partners, to cut that back and forth.

Inside Airtel, every report on the app went out by email, assembled by hand. I designed an analytics portal so Airtel’s stakeholders could check users, content ingestion and other metrics themselves. The first version showed too much at once; both our team and Airtel said it would confuse people. I rebuilt it around cards with headline figures and charts. That version was agreed, built, and used by Airtel’s team.

05

Outcome

By 2020 Airtel TV had more than 450,000 registered users in its first three markets, Nigeria, Zambia and Uganda, with 11 more African countries to follow.
