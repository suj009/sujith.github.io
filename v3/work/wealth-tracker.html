Wealth Tracker: from PRD to Flutter-ready

A PRD and an AI-generated concept in; approved, Flutter-ready web and mobile out. I took it end to end, into the repository and the environment, to close the gap between what design intends and what ships.

  • RoleEnd-to-end product and implementation ownership
  • PlatformsWeb and mobile, Flutter-ready
  • Front-end effortAbout 70% less, the front-end team’s estimate
  • What it led toThe Agentic Design Framework
01

The starting point

Wealth Tracker came from FYERS product leadership. The Product Owner shared a PRD and an early concept that already existed as an HTML output generated with AI. My starting point was an AI-generated representation that needed to be evaluated, challenged and turned into a coherent FYERS product.

The product consolidates a person’s investment picture: overall wealth, asset allocation, portfolio performance, holdings, and the insights that sit on top of them.

02

Review, challenge, refine

I read the PRD against the concept and asked the ordinary questions. Did it meet the requirement? Where were the UX and UI gaps? Did the hierarchy and interactions hold? What needed clarifying with the PO, and where did it diverge from FYERS patterns?

Then I worked the loop: find a gap, resolve the decision with the PO, direct the change, review the output, repeat. Execution got fast enough that judgement became the bottleneck, which is the right bottleneck.

The refined experience went through product review with the AVP of Product, the CPO and the CTO, and one more round of iteration before approval.

03

Changing my role in delivery

A normal workflow ends at handoff and picks up again at design review. After approval I went the other way. I joined the development environment, met the developers who owned the UI system, set up a separate repository for the implementation work, and set up the local environment myself.

I delivered the web experience first, reviewing a running product rather than static screens. Once the web workflow was done and I had analysed how it went, I did mobile the same way.

04

The outcome

Based on feedback from the front-end team, the approach was estimated to cut around 70% of the front-end implementation effort for this work. That is their estimate, not a measured benchmark, and I state it that way on purpose.

The saving was not only hours. The old process ran design and implementation reviews roughly every two weeks, and each round risked the design drifting in interpretation. Working closer to implementation removed most of the places where intent could change on its way to the user.

I didn’t open by proposing a framework. I did the work myself first, and the work made the argument.

05

Why it mattered beyond the feature

Taking one product from requirement to Flutter-ready output gave stakeholders enough confidence to ask a bigger question. It moved from can a designer do this? to can we make it repeatable? That question became the Agentic Design Framework.

The hard part was never generating an interface. It was deciding what should change, what should stay, what was missing and what met the bar. AI output is a starting point; the judgement is the job.
