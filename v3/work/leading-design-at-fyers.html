Leading design at FYERS: from a relay to owners

I lead product design across seven verticals at an Indian brokerage with over a million customers. The job is less the screens than the system that produces them: who owns a problem, how work is reviewed, and how far one person can carry it.

The problem
Seven verticals of a trading platform that ships fast, with design run as a relay: UX, then UI, then front-end, losing intent at every handoff.
My role
Senior Product Design Manager, reporting to the AVP of Product: direction, review and delivery oversight. A team of five designers today, eight at its largest.
The call
Organise around owners, not handoffs: one designer per problem, and now, with AIDA, one owner all the way to code.
The result
33 features shipped since February 2024, nine of them flagship launches, and AIDA rolling out to every product designer and product manager.
01

The remit

FYERS is an Indian brokerage with over a million customers. I lead product design across seven of its verticals as Senior Product Design Manager, reporting to the AVP of Product. My own team is five designers today and was eight at its largest, inside a design organisation that grew to 28 people.

Since February 2024 the team has shipped 33 features on web and app, about one a month: nine flagship launches, among them Smart Orders, FYERS Automate, Stock Screeners, Fixed Deposits and Strategy Builder, plus eight features and sixteen enhancements. I set the direction, run review and hold delivery oversight; the team owns execution.

02

From a relay to owners

When I joined FYERS, design ran like a relay. A requirement went to a UX designer for structure and flows, then to a UI designer to make it look finished, then to front-end to build. Three roles, three handoffs, and a little of the original intent lost at every one.

The call

Argue for one Product Designer role: one person carrying a problem from user need to a shipped screen, instead of a relay from UX to UI to front-end.

Why
Every handoff is a translation, and every translation loses something. One owner keeps the context in one head, and no one can say a problem was the other role’s job.
The trade-off
Less deep expertise at the edges. Designers grow into both structure and surface, and specialists are kept for the genuinely deep problems.

I joined as a UX Manager and became a Product Design Manager as this model took hold.

The relay race was never serving the user. It was serving the org chart.

03

Two organisational changes

I joined under the CXO, leading design for three verticals: Trading, Options and Onboarding.

The first change came in May 2025, when design moved into the Product organisation. I moved across with eight designers and took on every vertical, reporting directly to the CPO and co-founder.

The second came six months later, when the visual designers joined Product too. To keep the load even, Onboarding and Mutual Funds went to another Design Manager, and I kept seven verticals: Trading, Options, Automate, FIA, Markets, Institutional and Reports. Today I report to the AVP of Product.

04

How work gets to release

Direction, review and delivery oversight are mine; execution is the team’s.

Review runs two ways. Designers bring a blocker to me as soon as they hit it, so nothing waits for a meeting, and every piece of work gets a final review when the design is done. A stand-up every other day keeps me across where each designer stands.

Every design is reviewed twice: first by its product manager, then by me. I prefer the designer to walk me through it in person. The feedback goes into Figma as comments, or into notes for the next AI-generated version.

I also set one bar every design has to clear before it reaches me:

  • Every state designed: empty, loading, error and edge cases, not just the happy path.
  • Built from the design system: shared components, no one-off styles.
  • Web and app together: both platforms designed at once, not one after the other.
  • Real data: real prices, symbols and long values, not placeholder text.

Since then, developers come back with fewer questions during build, and reviews with me are faster, because the basics are already covered.

05

Growing the team

We ran two in-house hiring drives as the design organisation grew to 28 people.

I ran the second round. The candidates I put forward went on to a final round with the CXO, who made the offers.

06

Part of a bigger change

In August 2025 FYERS launched the all-new FYERS experience on the app, with the web following in September: its first push to unify web and app on one design system. I was part of the design team behind it, by then leading product design across every vertical.

07

Scaling myself

I still take on selected work myself. I designed FYERS’s first unified Orders section, every screen on web and app, and took Wealth 360 from a PRD to Flutter-ready web and mobile with around 70% less front-end effort, by the front-end team’s estimate. It is how I know what I am asking for when I ask for it.

The call

Turn that way of working into AIDA, a framework the whole team can use, and roll it out person by person.

Why
One designer doing this is a story; a team doing it every week is a capability. AIDA takes designers and product managers from idea to Flutter code.
The trade-off
Slower than announcing it to everyone at once. It was proven on Wealth 360, one product manager uses it on their own work today, and it is moving to every product designer and product manager one person at a time.
