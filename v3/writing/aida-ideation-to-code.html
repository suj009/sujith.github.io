AIDA: from one designer to a whole team

Wealth 360 proved one designer could take a product from idea to Flutter code. AIDA is how I am making that the way a whole design and product team works.

Sujith Kumar AnandAbout 4 min read

When I took Wealth 360 from a PRD to Flutter-ready web and mobile myself, working with an AI pair, the front-end team estimated it cut around 70% of the front-end effort. It was a good result and a poor template. A result that depends on one person is a story, not a capability.

So the question I took back to the team was not "can a designer do this?" but "can we make it repeatable?" AIDA is my answer: an internal agentic design framework I am leading, which gives designers and product managers the ability to go from ideation to Flutter code.

Why a framework, not a tool

Hand people an AI model and tell them to go faster, and you get fast inconsistency. What made Wealth 360 work was never the model. It was the judgement around it: a clear requirement, decisions settled with the product owner, approval from product and technology leadership before anything was built, and review against a running product instead of a picture of one.

A framework is how judgement travels to people who were not in the room.

Designers and product managers, on purpose

AIDA is for both. The handoff between product and design is as old, and as leaky, as the one between design and engineering. When a product manager can turn an idea into something real enough to test, the first design conversation starts from evidence instead of a document. When a designer can carry a decision into code, intent stops leaking on its way to the user.

It does not replace engineering. Architecture, performance and the hard systems problems stay where they belong. What changes is how much of the product arrives at engineering already decided.

Rolling it out slowly, on purpose

The temptation with a new way of working is to announce it to everyone at once. I am doing the opposite. AIDA was proven on one product first, Wealth 360. One product manager now uses it on their own work. From there it is moving, person by person, to every product designer and product manager.

Slow is the point. Each new person finds the places where the framework assumes too much, and each fix makes it sturdier for the next. A way of working that survives contact with many different people is worth more than one that impressed a room once.

What I would tell another design leader

  1. Prove it on real work before you name it. Wealth 360 came first and AIDA was named after it. The work made the argument; the framework followed.
  2. Keep the judgement human. AI output is a starting point. Deciding what should change, what should stay and what meets the bar is still the job.
  3. Bring product with you. A framework only designers use just moves the handoff somewhere else.
  4. Let the people who build do the measuring. The 70% figure is the front-end team’s estimate, not mine, and I call it an estimate. That is the only kind of number worth trusting.

AIDA is early: one product, one product manager, and a team on the way. But the question has already moved, from whether a designer can do this to how quickly a whole team can.
