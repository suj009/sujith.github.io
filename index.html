Strategy & Leadership · Design systems

FDSG — one design language across a fintech platform

Design Review Board member → owner of v2.0 & v2.1 · web + mobile · 8-person team

When I joined FYERS, design didn't have one language — it had several dialects. Style guides were scattered, half-finished, or duplicated, so the same element existed in different ways across web, iOS, and Android. That cost the business twice: designers rebuilt instead of designed, and users met a product that felt subtly inconsistent — costly in a product built on trust.

I became one of the louder voices for a single, versioned source of truth rather than more scattered guides. After v1.0, I took over and drove FDSG 2.0 and 2.1 across web and mobile — a visual reset from gradient-heavy to clean and minimal, then a pass that tightened spacing and density. The deeper change was organizational: I helped move FYERS from a split UX/UI relay to end-to-end Product Designers, which made adoption fast because the people using the system understood it top to bottom.

When engineering built it into code, we were honest that design-to-code was never a 100% match — naming the gap is what kept the system improving instead of quietly rotting.

Execution & Impact · Real-time trading

Options Scalper Terminal — designing for the hardest user in the building

Design lead & oversight · Options vertical · Q2 2024, ~3 months

Most fintech design makes the complex feel simple. This was the opposite: the user wants complexity, at speed, with zero margin for error. High-frequency options traders live in seconds — a slow interaction or an ambiguous state isn't an annoyance, it's money.

I set the direction and defended the standard: a fixed layout that becomes muscle memory, deeper analytics behind progressive disclosure so the default view stays scannable, live state read through colour and motion rather than full redraws, and error-prevention at speed — a deliberate confirm step and guardrails against fat-finger mistakes. The principle I held against pressure to show everything: the terminal should disappear into muscle memory.

The result showed up in the behaviour of the most demanding traders on the platform — they leaned in, spending more time in the tool and placing more through it.

About

The human, the credentials, and what it's actually like to work with me.

I'm a product design leader with over eleven years turning complex problems into products people enjoy — most of it in fintech, where the stakes and the trust are both high. I came into design from an engineering foundation (a master's in Advanced Computer Science before one in Human-Computer Interaction), which is exactly why "a design leader who ships front-end" isn't a stretch for me — it's where I started.

Today I lead product design at FYERS across six verticals — a team I built from five to a peak of eight, then right-sized back to five after a reorg, covering the same six verticals with fewer people than at the peak. Before that, fintech at Two Roads Tech, telecom and streaming at Datami, and Web3 at Corvell Labs; and before design was my job, I was the person running the interviews and diary studies as an HCI researcher at Microsoft and IBM.

What it's like to work with me

I optimize for clarity and ownership. I'd rather my team own a problem end to end — from user need to shipped screen — than pass it down a relay, and I try to lead the way I wish I'd been led: a clear direction, room to do the best work of your career, and systems that make the next project easier. I push for craft, I'll get into the code when it helps, and I care more about the team getting sharper than about being the smartest person in the room.
