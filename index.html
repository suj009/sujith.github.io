FDSG — one design language across a fintech platform

Design Review Board member → owner of v2.0 & v2.1 · web + mobile · 8-person team

When I joined FYERS, design didn't have one language — it had several dialects. The same element existed in different ways across web, iOS and Android, which cost the business twice: designers rebuilt instead of designed, and users met a product that felt subtly inconsistent.

I pushed for a single, versioned source of truth. After v1.0 I took over and drove FDSG 2.0 and 2.1 — a visual reset from gradient-heavy to clean and minimal, then a pass that tightened spacing and density until the components stopped fighting each other.

Execution & Impact · Real-time trading

Options Scalper Terminal — designing for the hardest user in the building

Design lead & oversight · Options vertical · Q2 2024, ~3 months

Most fintech design makes the complex feel simple. This was the opposite: the user wants complexity, at speed, with zero margin for error. High-frequency options traders live in seconds — a slow interaction or an ambiguous state isn't an annoyance, it's money.

I set the direction and defended the standard: a fixed layout that becomes muscle memory, analytics behind progressive disclosure so the default view stays scannable, live state read through colour and motion rather than full redraws, and error-prevention at speed — a deliberate confirm step and guardrails against fat-finger mistakes.

About

The human, the credentials, and what it's actually like to work with me.

I'm a product design leader with over ten years turning complex problems into products people enjoy — most of it in fintech, where the stakes and the trust are both high. I came into design from an engineering foundation (a master's in Advanced Computer Science before one in Human-Computer Interaction), which is exactly why "a design leader who ships front-end" isn't a stretch for me — it's where I started.

Today I lead product design at FYERS across six verticals. Before that, DeFi and fintech at Qplum, Datami, Nexter, and Oddz; and before design was my job, I was the person running the interviews and diary studies as an HCI researcher at Microsoft and IBM.

What it's like to work with me

I optimize for clarity and ownership. I'd rather my team own a problem end to end — from user need to shipped screen — than pass it down a relay, and I try to lead the way I wish I'd been led: a clear direction, room to do the best work of your career, and systems that make the next project easier. I push for craft, I'll get into the code when it helps, and I care more about the team getting sharper than about being the smartest person in the room.
