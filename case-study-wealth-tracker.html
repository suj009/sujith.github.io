Wealth Tracker — from PRD to Flutter-ready

A PRD and an AI-generated HTML concept in; approved, Flutter-ready web and mobile out. I took it end to end, including the repository and the environment, because the interesting part was closing the gap between what design intends and what ships.

FYERS · Reports Product Design Manager · end-to-end ownership 2026
Role
End-to-end product and implementation ownership
Platforms
Web and mobile, Flutter-ready
Front-end effort
~70% less — the front-end team's estimate
What it led to
The Agentic Design Framework, now in progress

The starting point was not a blank canvas

Wealth Tracker came from FYERS product leadership. The Product Owner shared a PRD and an early concept that already existed as an HTML output generated with Claude. So my starting point was an AI-generated representation that needed to be evaluated, challenged and turned into a coherent FYERS product.

The product itself was meant to consolidate a user's investment picture — overall wealth, asset allocation, portfolio performance, holdings, and the opportunities and insights that sit on top of them.

AI generated a starting point. Product and design judgement determined what happened next.

Review, challenge, refine

I read the PRD against the HTML and asked the ordinary questions: did it meet the requirement, were there UX or UI gaps, did the hierarchy and the interactions hold, what needed clarifying with the PO, and where did it diverge from FYERS patterns. Then I worked the loop — identify a gap, resolve the decision with the PO, direct the change, review the output, repeat.

I used Claude Cowork for the early iterations and brought in Claude Design once it launched. The tool is not the interesting part. What mattered was that execution got fast enough that judgement became the bottleneck — which is the right bottleneck.

Approval

The refined experience went through PRB review with the AVP – Product, the CPO and the CTO, and through another round of iteration before approval.

Then I changed my role in the delivery process

A normal workflow ends at Figma handoff and picks up again at design review. After approval I went the other way: I connected with the development team, got access to the development GitHub environment, met the developers who owned the UI system, set up a separate repository for the implementation work, and did the local environment setup myself.

I was no longer writing instructions for implementation. I was working in the implementation environment.

Flutter-ready output, web then mobile

I delivered the web experience first. Running it locally meant I was reviewing a rendered product rather than static screens — design intent, implementation-oriented output, run it, look at the real thing, fix what was wrong, go again. After the web workflow was complete and I had analysed how it went, I did the mobile experience the same way.

The outcome

Based on feedback from the front-end team, the approach was estimated to reduce around 70% of the front-end implementation effort for this work. That figure is their estimate, not a measured benchmark, and I state it that way deliberately.

The saving was not only hours. The previous process ran repeated design and implementation reviews on roughly a bi-weekly cadence, and each round carried the risk of interpretation drift. Working closer to implementation cut the number of places where design intent could change on its way to the user.

The goal was not to save effort. It was to reduce the distance between design intent and the final user experience.

Why it mattered beyond the feature

Taking one product from requirement to Flutter-ready output myself gave stakeholders enough confidence to ask a bigger question. It moved from can a designer use AI to produce implementation-oriented output to can we make that repeatable.

I am now leading an internal Agentic Design Framework to answer it — taking product teams from requirement toward implementation-ready output while keeping product, design and engineering review in the loop. It is a work in progress, and I am making no impact claims for it until it ships and is measured.

What I learned

The hard part was never generating an interface. It was deciding what should change, what should stay, what was missing, what needed product clarification, and what met the bar. AI output is a starting point; the judgement is the job.

The other lesson was about proof. I did not open by proposing a framework. I did the work myself first, and the work made the argument.
